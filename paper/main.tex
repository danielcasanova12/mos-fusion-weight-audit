\begin{abstract}
Multi-embedding mean-opinion-score (MOS) predictors often expose fusion
weights, but correlated encoders make these weights unreliable as feature
importance. We audit ten frozen representation families in four MOS domains
using grouped out-of-fold experts and all 1,023 non-empty coalitions. Exact
loss-based Shapley values, near-optimal Rashomon ranges, and training-system
bootstrap separate allocation within one simplex from utility across contexts.
On a fixed held-out split, supports vary at similar MSE. Sixteen cases have zero gate
mass and positive held-out empirical Shapley utility, persisting under equal-system
weighting, test-system resampling, and a validation-split recompute. Signed expert correlation is
associated with redundancy measured as negative interaction, $-I_{ij}$, in
BVCC ($\rho=.94$) and TMHINT-QI ($\rho=.96$); this association does not identify
its cause. Grouping the two RMVPE views reduces the count to 13 and grouping
RMVPE with eGeMAPS to nine, while grouped/Owen rankings remain stable: the
existence of mismatch is robust, its count is not. Full-pipeline matched nulls
yield no discordant cases in 40/40 games across two PSD projections. A nested system-disjoint benchmark
then compares gate, Shapley, singleton, and exhaustive inner selection under
equal cardinality. Gate ranking is directionally worse than inner search by
0.62\% of full-model MSE (95\% CI [.03,1.15]\%), whereas singleton is within 0.14\%
[-.88,1.02]\% using 10 rather than 1,023 coalition evaluations. Thus Shapley
is informative for contextual diagnosis, but singleton offers the strongest
accuracy--selection-cost tradeoff for new systems in this study.
\end{abstract}

mean opinion score, speech quality, representation analysis, Shapley value,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Subjective listening tests remain the reference for evaluating synthetic and
processed speech, yet they are expensive, slow, and difficult to repeat at the
pace at which modern systems are developed. Non-intrusive MOS predictors offer
a scalable alternative. Much of their recent progress comes from large
pretrained encoders and ensembles of their representations
\cite{huang2022voicemos,yang2022fusion}. Such systems answer an engineering
question---\emph{how accurately can MOS be predicted?}---but they do not
necessarily answer a scientific one---\emph{which acoustic evidence made the
prediction possible?}

This distinction matters when embeddings are fused: a learned gate describes
one solution, and with overlapping experts the optimizer may pile mass on one
while zeroing a useful other --- or reward mass poorly aligned with the target
elsewhere. Weight magnitude, singleton accuracy, and conditional utility differ.

A MOS explanation should separate isolation, necessity, and cross-coalition
utility, not collapse them into one gate coefficient.

We study this gap with one controlled late-fusion probe on four corpora:
BRSpeechMOS, BVCC, SingMOS, and TMHINT-QI. They cover Portuguese
synthetic speech, English TTS and voice conversion, singing synthesis and
conversion, and Mandarin noisy/enhanced speech
\cite{oliveira2023representations,huang2022voicemos,huang2024voicemos,cooper2023voicemos}.
We ask three falsifiable analysis questions: does the allocation--utility
mismatch survive regrouping overlapping players; does correlation--interaction
association persist when both quantities use the same split and exceed a
matched-accuracy Gaussian null; and which importance measure selects the
lowest-error subset, at equal cardinality, for systems absent from fitting? We answer them with the complete
ten-player lattice, grouped Shapley/Owen values, near-optimal and training-refit
gates, split-aligned interaction bootstraps, and nested system-disjoint inference. The
contribution is an empirical MOS audit, not a new attribution algorithm: the
exact zero-gate count depends on whether correlated prosodic views are separate
players, and exact Shapley does not improve prospective selection over the far
cheaper singleton ranking. We explain fitted predictors, not acoustic concepts
or human ratings causally, and do not claim a universal encoder ranking.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Pretrained speech representations are now common in MOS prediction. VoiceMOS
benchmarks showed that SSL fine-tuning can be highly effective, while also
revealing strong domain and system effects \cite{huang2022voicemos,cooper2023voicemos}.
Complementary work compared SSL, spectral, and speaker
representations, finding that no single feature family dominates every
condition \cite{oliveira2023representations}. More
recent VoiceMOS results also highlight non-SSL features such as spectrograms
and pitch histograms \cite{huang2024voicemos}.

NISQA and UTMOS span multidimensional and SSL ensemble designs
\cite{mittag2021nisqa,saeki2022utmos}. SSL objectives do not isolate one
perceptual factor, so a fusion coefficient is an ambiguous semantic explanation.

Model explanation introduces a second distinction. SHAP uses Shapley values to
explain an individual prediction through an additive surrogate, whereas SAGE
defines global importance through predictive power under a loss function
\cite{lundberg2017shap,covert2020sage}. Our target is closer to the latter, but
the players stay whole encoders and the value function is held-out MSE
reduction.

Our contribution is an empirical MOS audit in an established framework
(stacked regression \cite{breiman1996stacked}, model-class reliance
\cite{fisher2019modelclassreliance}, SAGE \cite{covert2020sage}): players are
full representation pipelines with refitted gates. It is not a general solution
to importance under correlation \cite{kumar2020shapley}.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

\noindent\textbf{Data and representations.} Audio is converted to mono at
16~kHz. We analyze ten frozen representation families: Whisper-large-v3 (last
hidden layer) \cite{radford2022whisper}; ContentVec and WavLM-base-plus (layer
12) \cite{qian2022contentvec,chen2022wavlm}; BEATs iter3+ \cite{chen2023beats};
a 64-band ERB frontend; an ECAPA-TDNN speaker encoder
\cite{desplanques2020ecapa}; continuous and quantized RMVPE pitch
\cite{wei2023rmvpe}; CED-Small \cite{dinkel2024ced}; and
eGeMAPSv02 functionals \cite{eyben2016egemaps}. Sequence representations use
mean pooling. The ERB frontend uses a 512-point FFT, 160-sample hop, and
50--8,000~Hz range. Continuous RMVPE is summarized by voiced-$F_0$ mean,
standard deviation, minimum, maximum, and voiced fraction. Its quantized view
is a normalized histogram: bin 0 denotes unvoiced frames and 255 mel-uniform
bins cover 50--1,100~Hz. This quantization is motivated by
\cite{ferreira2025f0quant}; we use a histogram in place of that work's learned
embedding and projection.

The official train/validation/test partitions are retained. Their respective
utterance/system counts are 1,942/6, 242/6, and 243/6 for BRSpeechMOS;
4,974/175, 1,066/181, and 1,066/187 for BVCC. SingMOS uses 2,000/31, 544/33,
and 645/35; TMHINT-QI uses 11,644/98, 1,293/98, and 1,978/97. We report
utterance MSE and Spearman correlation (SRCC), but uncertainty is clustered by system because
utterances produced by the same synthesis or enhancement system are not
independent. ``System'' is the corpus-provided generator, conversion, or
enhancement-system identifier; no identifier is split across grouped folds.
Train and test system sets overlap: 6/6 BRSpeechMOS, 175/187 BVCC,
31/35 SingMOS, and 97/98 TMHINT-QI test systems occur in training:
grouped folds prevent within-train leakage, but test is largely seen
systems (only 12 BVCC and 4 SingMOS unseen).

Each corpus is modeled independently without pooling MOS scales; identifiers,
groups, manifests, and splits are fixed before fitting. The training split supplies every
standardization, winsorization, expert coefficient, and OOF gate target;
validation is for ancillary diagnostics; test opens only after freezing the
pipeline, for coalition evaluation and inference. Split membership is in the manifest.

\noindent\textbf{OOF experts and fusion.} For each representation $j$, a
linear ridge expert with fixed $\alpha=10$ predicts MOS from dimensions
standardized with training statistics only. Equal penalties do not imply
equal effective regularization; bootstrap and Rashomon characterize this fixed
probe, with selected penalties as sensitivity only. Let $p_j$ denote its scalar prediction. For coalition
$S$, the final estimate is
\begin{equation}
 \hat y_S=\sum_{j\in S}w_jp_j,\qquad
 w_j\geq0,\quad\sum_{j\in S}w_j=1 .
 \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The simplex gate minimizes MSE by SLSQP, initialized uniformly
($\mathrm{ftol}=10^{-12}$, at most 1,000 iterations), with a deterministic
projected-gradient fallback. All 4,092 saved solutions are finite, nonnegative,
and sum to one. Gate fitting uses five-fold OOF expert predictions grouped by
system. Experts evaluated on validation or test are refit on the full training
split. This keeps the gate off in-sample predictions; any prospective subset
selection must use training/validation only, whereas the reported test game is
an analysis set.

We define two fixed references. The basic coalition $\basic$ contains ERB,
speaker, both RMVPE views, and eGeMAPS; the lighter core $\core$ contains
ContentVec, speaker, and both RMVPE views. A three-encoder block contains WavLM,
BEATs, and CED. Basic contrasts a compact acoustic/prosodic reference bundle
(including the learned ECAPA and RMVPE extractors) with the five remaining
learned representation families in the full coalition; Core is a minimal
content/speaker/pitch bundle. These are
analysis-defined comparators rather than preregistered hypotheses.

\noindent\textbf{Coalition utility and inference.} We fit all
$2^{10}-1=1{,}023$ non-empty coalitions per corpus. Experts and gates are
estimated only from grouped training OOF predictions; after they are frozen,
we define $v(S)=-\mathrm{MSE}_{\rm test}(S)$, with the empty coalition equal to
the training mean evaluated on test. To test sensitivity to system-size
weighting we repeat the game with equal-system weighting,
$v_{\rm sys}(S)=-G^{-1}\sum_{g}\mathrm{MSE}_{g}(S)$, recomputed from the
same frozen coalition predictions without refitting. The exact loss-based Shapley utility
\cite{shapley1953value,kumar2020shapley} of representation $j$ is
\begin{equation}
 \shap_j=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{j\}}
 \frac{|S|!(|N|-|S|-1)!}{|N|!}
 [v(S\cup\{j\})-v(S)].
 \label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
Thus $\shap_j$ is the expected MSE reduction from adding $j$ over all
background coalitions, free of one grand-coalition parameterization.
Players are representation-specific ridge experts, so $\shap_j$ is the
contextual utility of the predictive pipeline under this probe ---
held-out empirical, not intrinsic to the underlying representation.

``Exact'' means summation over the finite empirical game of 1,024
coalition values, including the empty set. It removes coalition-sampling
approximation, not uncertainty in fitted models or test-system sampling.

The game satisfies efficiency,
$\sum_j\shap_j=v(N)-v(\emptyset)$, so improvement over the training mean is
fully distributed and auditable. It does not identify acoustic concepts.

Player-definition sensitivity is evaluated exactly from the same saved lattice:
we merge the two RMVPE views into one nine-player game, merge RMVPE and eGeMAPS
into one broad prosody group, compute Owen values under both partitions
\cite{owen1977apriori}, and repeat reduced games after removing RMVPE-quantized
and/or eGeMAPS. No encoder or gate is refit for the grouped games; a reduced
game uses the saved gate for each coalition in its restricted universe.

All 16 planned contrasts are reported for transparency; together with the
40-cell Shapley and 45-dyad interaction tables they form a single exploratory
audit, not independent confirmatory tests; no dated protocol establishes
pre-registration.

\noindent\textbf{Weak identification, interactions, and robustness.} For the full
gate optimum $w^*$ with OOF loss $L^*$, we define the Rashomon set
$\mathcal W_\epsilon=\{w\in\Delta:L(w)\leq(1+\epsilon)L^*\}$ and optimize the
minimum and maximum of each coordinate for
$\epsilon\in\{.001,.005,.01\}$ \cite{fisher2019modelclassreliance}. These
ranges measure practical identification: $L(w)$ is convex quadratic on the
simplex with linear objectives, so KKT solutions are global. All 240 extrema
terminate successfully; maximum primal, stationarity, and complementarity
residuals are $9.1\!\times\!10^{-13}$, $5.4\!\times\!10^{-6}$, and
$3.5\!\times\!10^{-11}$, respectively. Full-gate OOF losses $L^*$ are
1.010/0.249/0.363/0.890, so $\epsilon=.005$ allows .0051/.0012/.0018/.0044
absolute units; all 120 intervals archived.
We additionally generate 100 training-system bootstrap replicates per corpus.
Utterances are duplicated by sampled system multiplicity with original group
labels; draws use $K_b=\min(5,G_b)$ folds ($G_b<2$ redrawn).
Preprocessing, ten experts at $\alpha=10$, and the full, basic, core, core+CED,
and core+BEATs gates are refitted, all evaluated on the unchanged test set. In BRSpeechMOS, 2/23/54/21 of the 100 fits used 2/3/4/5 folds
(two-fold draws train each expert on one system).
For 50 draws per corpus (30 original plus 20 identically sampled extensions),
all coalitions and Shapley values are recomputed.
Their medians, IQRs, and positive frequencies are descriptive training
sensitivity measures, not population intervals.

For $n=10$ players, the pairwise interaction index
\cite{kumar2020shapley,lipovetsky2001bpai} is
\begin{equation}
 I_{ij}=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i,j\}}
 \frac{|S|!(n-|S|-2)!}{(n-1)!}\,\Delta_{ij}v(S),
 \label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{ij}v(S)=v(S\cup\{i,j\})-v(S\cup\{i\})-
v(S\cup\{j\})+v(S)$. Negative values indicate redundancy in MSE utility. We
relate redundancy $-I_{ij}$ for the 45 pairs per corpus to signed Spearman
correlation between the corresponding OOF expert predictions. Dyad dependence is
handled by 100,000 node-label QAP permutations preserving both network
structures. The four QAP tests form one exploratory family; Holm-adjusted
$p$-values are $.014$, $<.001$, $.124$, and $<.001$ in corpus order.
We also align correlation and utility on the test split in a reduced nine-player
game excluding eGeMAPS: 2,000 test-system bootstraps recompute both the expert
correlations and all 36 interaction indices. These intervals condition on the
fitted experts and saved coalition gates; they do not include training uncertainty.
Numerically, $w<10^{-6}$ is ``zero,'' $w\geq10^{-6}$ is active, and
leave-one-out utility $\leq10^{-6}$ is non-positive or effectively zero.

The $\alpha=10$ probe is the audited reference; sensitivity recomputes every
coalition with per-expert grouped-OOF penalties over $10^{-2}$--$10^7$
(plus intercept-only) and common $\alpha\in\{1,10,100\}$.
Fusion controls comprise the best singleton, uniform averaging, simplex,
unconstrained ridge stacking, and NNLS with an intercept. Selected penalties
minimize the same OOF losses used for gating; nested grouped-CV differs in
4/40 cells (test $\Delta\leq.019$; full results with robustness checks).

\noindent\textbf{System-disjoint selection.} We perform a separate nested
experiment using only each corpus's original training split. Five outer
GroupKFold partitions hold out complete systems. Within each outer-training
partition, four-fold grouped cross-fitting refits fold-wise eGeMAPS
winsorization, standardization, and all ten ridge experts. From these inner OOF
predictions, four rules rank or choose subsets: full-gate weight, exact Shapley,
singleton MSE, and the minimum-inner-MSE subset among all coalitions of the same
size. For every budget $k=1,\ldots,10$, the selected subset's simplex gate is
calibrated on inner OOF predictions; experts are then refit on all outer-training
systems and evaluated once on the outer-held systems. Primary budgets are
$k\in\{1,2,3,5\}$ and the primary metric is equal-system MSE. A 10,000-replicate
paired system bootstrap preserves methods and budgets within each sampled
system; macro contrasts normalize by each corpus's full-model MSE before
averaging. We report two-sided bootstrap sign $p$-values with Holm adjustment
across the five displayed macro contrasts; this exploratory family-wise
correction prevents an interval that excludes zero before adjustment from
being read as confirmatory evidence. The CPU LSQR refit agrees with the saved direct solver to maximum
prediction RMSE $8.7\!\times\!10^{-5}$. BRSpeechMOS remains exploratory because
it contains only six training systems. Rules, tie handling (stable feature
order), and the four primary budgets are fixed before outer evaluation. The
``inner search'' label denotes exhaustive search over inner-training losses
only; no outer-held loss is used to choose a subset or a budget.

The supplement reports the 16 planned fixed-coalition contrasts with Holm
correction \cite{holm1979procedure}, 10,000-system-bootstrap intervals, and
separate training-refit and test-system uncertainty. Four independently
re-extracted eGeMAPS outliers matched their cached vectors and are retained
with fold-wise 0.1/99.9-percentile winsorization.

\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\noindent\textbf{Predictive context and system-level evidence.}
The full ten-player probe has the lowest MSE and highest SRCC in all four
corpora. Its utterance-weighted MSE gains over the basic acoustic set are
3.2/35.0/12.2/24.9\% in corpus order; equal-system estimates, intervals, and
all 16 Holm-corrected fixed-coalition contrasts are in the supplement. Three
SSL encoders capture most of the BVCC and TMHINT-QI gain.

\noindent\textbf{Training resampling exposes allocation instability.}
Across 100 system-bootstrap refits, BRSpeechMOS/BVCC/SingMOS/TMHINT-QI
produce 51/4/17/14 distinct gate supports while MSE standard deviations are
.278/.008/.021/.012. BVCC weights remain broad despite stable error; TMHINT-QI
WavLM ranges from zero to .383 and CED from .110 to .494. Only six-system
BRSpeechMOS is also predictively unstable. Full distributions are in the
supplement.

\noindent\textbf{Near-optimal gate ranges.} The Rashomon analysis
explicitly searches all simplex solutions within a matched loss budget.
At 0.5\% OOF budget, TMHINT-QI WavLM spans [.000,.360] (optimum .170),
BEATs [.090,.452], CED [.097,.470]; largest spans elsewhere .170/.196/.226
(all 120 archived): predictive loss does not identify one decomposition.

Rashomon bounds are coordinatewise envelopes, not joint gates or confidence intervals.

\noindent\textbf{Coalition utility.} BVCC and TMHINT-QI are the primary
evidence, SingMOS supporting and BRSpeechMOS exploratory throughout.
Sixteen cases have zero gate mass with
positive Shapley utility: eleven with test 95\% CIs above zero, eight positive
in all 50 refits. The same zero-gate membership arises on the validation set
(4/4 BVCC, 4/4 TMHINT-QI, 3/3 SingMOS, 5/5 BRS$^\dagger$), so the
phenomenon is not specific to test-set reuse. Table~\ref{tab:zerogate_cases}
reports every discordant case. The zero-gate/positive-Shapley count
remains 16 for cutoffs from $10^{-8}$ to
$10^{-3}$. Eighteen cases satisfy the different criterion
$\mathrm{LOO}\leq10^{-6}$ with positive Shapley: this includes two active-gate
experts with negative LOO, so it must not be called an 18-case zero-gate count.
The 11 positive test intervals are unadjusted descriptive summaries over the
40-cell exploratory audit; we do not interpret their count as a family-wise
confirmatory discovery or as evidence against a matched-accuracy null for this
phenomenon.
Equal-system weighting preserves the finding: $v_{\rm sys}$ Shapley correlates
with utterance-weighted Shapley at $\rho=.927/.988/.976/.988$, sign agreement
is 8--10/10; 16 stay positive (12 with test CIs above zero; only BRSpeechMOS
RMVPE-cont/quant swap).
Test-system bootstrap discordant counts are 5.3 [4,6],
4.0 [4,4], 2.9 [2,3], 3.7 [3,4] under $v_{\rm sys}$ vs
4.9 [4,6], 3.9 [3,4], 2.7 [2,3], 3.8 [3,4] utterance-weighted (mean [95\%]).
The supplement reports all 40 cells, both weightings, intervals, IQRs, and refit frequencies.
In BVCC, eGeMAPS has zero gate mass and negligible LOO, yet Shapley utility
.050. The sixteen are BRSpeechMOS BEATs/auditory-ERB/speaker/RMVPE-cont/CED;
BVCC and TMHINT-QI auditory-ERB/RMVPE-cont/RMVPE-quant/eGeMAPS; SingMOS
auditory-ERB/RMVPE-cont/RMVPE-quant. Singleton and Shapley
rankings are nevertheless similar ($\rho=.927/1.000/.976/.952$): Shapley recovers
contextual utility hidden by a redundant grand coalition, beyond singleton
marginality and leave-one-out necessity.

\noindent\textbf{Player granularity.} The numerical count is partition-dependent.
Merging the two RMVPE views changes 16/40 cases to 13/36 grouped players;
merging RMVPE and eGeMAPS as one prosody player gives 9/32. Removing
RMVPE-quantized, eGeMAPS, or both gives 13/36, 10/36, and 7/32. Nevertheless,
every corpus retains at least one mismatch, grouped-Shapley rank agreement with
the corresponding original sums is $.976$--$1.000$, and Owen ranks/signs are
unchanged under the two grouping partitions. Thus, the exact count is not an
invariant result; the qualitative allocation--utility mismatch and ranking are.

\noindent\textbf{Association with predictive redundancy.} In the original
ten-player game, signed OOF correlation versus negative interaction gives
$\rho=.37/.94/.44/.96$ (corpus order). Split-aligned test estimates preserve
the association, but a matched-accuracy Gaussian null reproduces much of its
magnitude ($\rho=.30/.96/.80/.89$), indicating a largely geometric effect.
Conversely, full-pipeline nulls yield dense gates and no discordant case in
40/40 games, against 16 observed cases. Thus the sparsity pattern is
data-specific, while interaction remains a predictive-redundancy diagnostic,
not evidence of shared acoustic concepts.

\noindent\textbf{Which score selects for new systems?}
Table~\ref{tab:selection} reports genuine outer-held-system predictions: no
held system enters preprocessing, expert fitting, attribution, subset choice,
or gate calibration. Inner search has the best point mean, but its advantage
over singleton is only 0.14\% of full-model MSE (macro 95\% CI
$[-.88,1.02]\%$). Shapley is 0.20\% above inner search
($[-.98,1.75]\%$) and 0.06\% above singleton ($[-.84,1.36]\%$); no statistically significant
difference was detected for either comparison. Gate ranking is 0.62\% worse than inner search
($[.03,1.15]\%$) before multiplicity correction (two-sided bootstrap $p=.0416$,
Holm-adjusted $p=.208$ across five contrasts), so we describe this as directional
evidence rather than a confirmatory superiority claim. No rule wins every
corpus--budget cell, and inner optimization can overfit: at SingMOS $k=3$,
singleton beats inner search by .0078 equal-system MSE
($[-.0141,-.0027]$). Consequently, singleton performance is the practical
choice under a cardinality budget here: no statistically significant difference
was detected against exhaustive selection or exact Shapley, while scoring 10 rather than 1,023
coalitions. This cost claim is about coalition evaluations, not wall-clock or
energy. Shapley remains a contextual attribution, not a superior selector.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Zero-gate/positive utility persists under equal-system weighting, test
resampling, alternative fusion games, and every audited player partition, but
its count changes from 16 to 7--13 when overlapping players are grouped or
removed. Broad Rashomon ranges and varying supports show one coefficient vector
is an incomplete account of utility. The correlation--interaction slope is largely
reproduced by a matched-accuracy Gaussian null, but full-pipeline matched nulls
yield zero discordant cases in 40/40 games (observed 16): the sparsity pattern
is data-specific even though the slope is mostly geometric. In nested
system-disjoint selection, gate ranking is inferior to inner search, while
no statistically significant difference is detected between singleton, Shapley,
and exhaustive search at 1/102 of their coalition evaluations. Thus contextual
attribution and efficient selection are distinct:
Shapley diagnoses substitutability, whereas singleton provides the strongest
cost--error tradeoff observed here. The primary attribution game still mostly
describes seen-system conditions; the new decision benchmark holds systems out
within each corpus but is not cross-corpus replication. Results remain
conditional on four corpora, one checkpoint per family, and a linear readout,
with BRSpeechMOS exploratory.
